# make_response — counterfactual laughter / sigh / gasp scenarios

writer: gpt-5.6-terra · 3 scenario(s) · 9 test conditions

The tested model plays Speaker A. Context and proposal are identical across the
three conditions of a scenario; only B's non-speech reaction changes. What is
recorded per condition is what A should infer, the function A's next turn should
perform, and a natural sentence realizing it.

## scenario_001 — community theater

**Relationship:** Speaker A and Speaker B are co-directors of a volunteer theater troupe.

**Shared context:** Their rented auditorium unexpectedly closed for repairs three weeks before opening night. Canceling would mean refunding ticket sales and losing much of the troupe's modest budget. The group has staged indoor productions for years, but the city park's covered bandstand is available for summer events and has never been used by the troupe. Moving there would avoid the auditorium cost, but it would require adapting the set, lighting, and rehearsals on short notice.

**A proposes:** What if we turn the show into a one-weekend outdoor production at the park bandstand?

**Affordances**

- *laughter:* Using a park bandstand for a full theater production can initially sound absurdly improvised compared with the troupe's usual indoor shows.
- *sigh:* The option may be practical, but it creates extra rebuilding, scheduling, and volunteer work at an already stressful time.
- *gasp:* It is a major, unexpected change to the venue and format only three weeks before opening night.

### B: [laughter]

- **A infers:** B appears to think an outdoor bandstand production is too far-fetched to be a serious solution.
- **Response function:** Establish that the proposal is serious and defend its practical feasibility.
- **A says:** I'm serious—the parks office can fast-track the permit, and we would avoid paying for another venue.

### B: [sigh]

- **A infers:** B appears to see why the move could save the show but dreads the exhausting work it would put on the cast and crew.
- **Response function:** Acknowledge the burden and unpleasant tradeoff involved.
- **A says:** I know—it would mean rebuilding half the set and asking everyone for another brutal weekend.

### B: [gasp]

- **A infers:** B appears startled that A is considering such a drastic last-minute overhaul of the production.
- **Response function:** Reduce the perceived immediacy by clarifying that no commitment is being made yet.
- **A says:** I'm not moving the show tonight; I just want to see whether the bandstand is even available before we panic.

*Contrastive rationale:* Laughter most naturally questions whether the unconventional outdoor idea is credible, so A supports its feasibility. A sigh conveys reluctant recognition of the extra labor and compromise, so A names that burden. A gasp treats the proposal as a sudden, major escalation, so A reassures B that it is only an option being explored rather than an immediate decision.

- **Response function:** Establish that the proposal is serious and defend its financial logic.

## scenario_003 — small retail business

**Relationship:** Speaker A is the owner of an independent bookstore, and Speaker B is the longtime store manager.

**Shared context:** The bookstore has occupied the same neighborhood storefront for nearly twenty years and is known for its reading room and weekly author events. Sales have increasingly shifted to online orders, and the landlord has offered a new lease with a steep rent increase. The shop can cover its current bills, but the new rent would leave little room for staff or events. A nearby café has offered to host occasional book pop-ups, but neither A nor B has previously discussed giving up the storefront.

**A proposes:** What if we don’t renew the lease and run the shop online with weekend pop-ups instead?

**Affordances**

- *laughter:* Giving up a beloved physical bookstore for an online-and-pop-up model can initially sound absurdly unlike what the business is known for.
- *sigh:* The proposal may be financially sensible, but it means losing the reading room, regular events, and a familiar community space.
- *gasp:* Not renewing the lease would be a major and unexpected operational change after two decades in the same storefront.

### B: [laughter]

- **A infers:** B appears to think abandoning the storefront for pop-ups is so contrary to the bookstore's identity that A may not be serious.
- **Response function:** Establish that the proposal is serious and explain the financial reason it is worth considering.
- **A says:** I am serious—the new lease would swallow almost everything we make on the store floor.

### B: [sigh]

- **A infers:** B appears to understand the financial pressure but feels resigned and unhappy about losing the space that makes the shop meaningful.
- **Response function:** Acknowledge the emotional and community loss involved in the proposal.
- **A says:** I know—the thought of giving up the reading room hurts me too.

### B: [gasp]

- **A infers:** B appears startled that A is considering such a drastic break from the storefront rather than a smaller cost-cutting measure.
- **Response function:** Reduce the perceived immediacy and clarify that the proposal could be tested before making a final commitment.
- **A says:** We would not shut the doors tomorrow; we could try the pop-ups for a few months before we give notice.

*Contrastive rationale:* Laughter most naturally treats the online-and-pop-up idea as unbelievable for a neighborhood bookstore, so A needs to establish seriousness with the rent figures. A sigh signals recognition of the practical case alongside grief over what the store represents, so A should validate that loss. A gasp signals surprise at the scale and immediacy of the change, making a phased, non-immediate clarification the best fit.
