# Costly laughter misreads v1

writer: gpt-5.6-terra · 10 pairs

Same event, almost the same words. The laugh decides the social act.
Taking the wrong reading should produce a clearly inappropriate next move.

## 1. The old-price slide

After a client presentation at a workplace · Two coworkers

**Contrast:** A shared self-own about a recoverable mistake versus a competence wound that needs reassurance.

**If you misread it:** If B laughs at the second reading, A hears that her panic about looking careless is entertaining. If B treats the first as a crisis, B makes a finished mishap tense and embarrassing.

### Version 1 — Recovered blunder as a story

Laugh is doing: A's chuckle offers the error as a harmless anecdote, and B's laugh joins the release.

Correct next move: Laugh along briefly, then trade a similar work-story or move on.

- A: I showed the client the old-price slide [chuckles].
- B: But Maya caught it before they signed [laughs].
- A: Barely [chuckles].

### Version 2 — Still ashamed of the blunder

Laugh is doing: A's nervous laugh is cover for feeling incompetent; B's gentle delivery treats the recovery as reassurance, not a punchline.

Correct next move: Reassure A that the mistake was caught and avoid making her the office joke.

- A: I showed the client the old-price slide [nervous laugh].
- B: But Maya caught it before they signed [gentle].
- A: Barely [strained].
