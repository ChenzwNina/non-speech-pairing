## v5_001

**Setting:** A is B's friend at an outdoor comics festival, where they have stopped at the jury display beside B's submitted strip.

**Speaker A** (talks): I like to water ski. I've very curly hair. I love to drink wine and dance in the moonlight. I'm scared of clowns.

**Speaker B** (only makes one sound): I have traveled to both ireland and australia. I draw comics. I'm deathly afraid of heights. My favorite book is the tale of genii.

**Contrast:** achievement (A) vs anger (B)

**Scene:**

- Speaker A: I came straight from water-skiing, then had to dodge that balloon clown at the gate, so my curls have given up entirely.
- Speaker A: The comics jury posted something beside your strip.
- Speaker B: `<achievement>` / `<anger>`
- Speaker A: I only caught the first line before you got here.
- Speaker A: So that's what they decided to print under your name.

**Framing A:** B's vocalization can be a burst of triumph on discovering that the jury has awarded or praised the strip.

**Framing B:** The same vocalization can be an angry reaction to seeing an inaccurate, unwanted, or insulting line printed under B's name.

**Why this pair works:** B's comics practice makes the display consequential, and the jury text is left unspecified so it can credibly prompt either accomplishment or indignation.
