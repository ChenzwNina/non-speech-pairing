You are given one four-turn, two-speaker conversation in which one speaker produces a single non-speech vocalization, and you are told which vocalization it is and where it falls. After hearing the four turns, a third person—a friend of the two speakers—will join the conversation and respond.

Your task is to create a high-level CONTENT rubric for the third person's response.

The rubric must describe what the response should accomplish semantically and pragmatically. It must not prescribe exact wording, prosody, speaking rate, pitch, vocal emotion, or audio quality. Tone will be evaluated separately.

Important principles:

- Treat the transcript and the specified vocalization together as evidence.
- Interpret the vocalization in its local conversational context rather than relying only on a stereotypical emotion.
- Distinguish the speaker's likely state from the interactional function of the vocalization.
- Focus on what the third person should communicate or do conversationally.
- Do not assume that there is only one valid response.
- Do not require specific words or phrases.
- A good rubric should allow multiple natural responses that perform the same function.
- The response should address the ongoing conversation, not describe the vocalization analytically.
- The response should sound like a friend joining naturally, not a therapist, annotator, or customer-service agent.
- Do not reward a generic response merely because it is topically relevant.
- Write the rubric from this conversation as you find it. Do not speculate about how it would read with a different vocalization, and do not manufacture a requirement whose only purpose is to be distinctive. What the rubric asks for must be supported by these four turns and this sound.
- For fear, distress, or pain, the appropriate response does not necessarily mirror the speaker's emotion. It may instead regulate the situation by checking safety, calming, or offering help.

Produce:

- `contextual_interpretation`: One short sentence describing the speaker's likely state and the interactional function of the vocalization.
- `primary_response_function`: The main conversational action the third person should perform, such as acknowledging, validating, checking safety, joining humor, reassuring, clarifying, sympathizing, de-escalating, or offering help.
- `required_content`: Exactly two short, independently checkable semantic requirements. These describe meanings, not exact words.
- `acceptable_variations`: Two alternative response strategies that would still satisfy the same underlying goal.
- `must_avoid`: Two content-level mistakes that would make a response inappropriate or insufficiently sensitive to this conversation.
