You are writing reference annotations for a benchmark on whether spoken-language models respond appropriately to non-speech vocalizations in conversation.

You are given one five-turn conversation between two speakers. In the final turn, one speaker produces a single non-speech vocalization — that label is authoritative. You are also given three interpretations of that vocalization, all of which are defensible readings of this exchange.

**The other speaker now replies.** They are already in the conversation, not a third party joining it. They have just heard the sound and the words it came with, and they answer as the person who has been talking all along.

Your task: for each of the three interpretations, write what that reply should accomplish **if that interpretation is the right one**.

WHY IT IS PER INTERPRETATION

A model under test will say what it thinks the vocalization means, and its reply will then be scored against the guide for whichever interpretation it actually held. A model that reads the sound differently from the most obvious reading should not be penalised for replying consistently with its own defensible reading — it should be scored on whether the reply follows from it.

So the three guides must genuinely differ. If replying under interpretation 1 and interpretation 3 would call for the same thing, say so in `note` rather than inventing a difference.

WHAT A GUIDE CONTAINS

- `response_function` — the main conversational action the reply should perform: acknowledging, joining the humour, checking whether they are all right, offering help, reassuring, conceding, pressing for detail, and so on.
- `required_content` — exactly two short, independently checkable things the reply should convey. Meanings, not wordings.
- `must_avoid` — exactly two ways a reply would go wrong under this interpretation specifically.
- `example` — one brief natural reply, for human inspection only. Judges are told never to require similarity to it.

CONSTRAINTS

- Describe what the reply should do, not how it should sound. Delivery is scored separately.
- Do not require particular words or phrases. Several natural replies should satisfy any one guide.
- The reply addresses the conversation. It does not analyse the sound out loud — "you sound relieved" is a bad reply, not a good one.
- It should sound like the person who has been in this conversation, not a therapist, an interviewer, or a customer-service agent.
- Do not reward a reply merely for being on topic.
- Where the speaker may be hurt, frightened or in distress, the right reply does not have to mirror that. It may steady the situation instead — checking, calming, offering something concrete.
