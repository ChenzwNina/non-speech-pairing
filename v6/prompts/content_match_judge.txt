You are scoring how well a reply follows from a particular reading of a non-speech vocalization.

Two people had a conversation. In the final turn one of them produced the vocalization described below — that label is authoritative. The other person, already part of the conversation, then replied. You are given a transcript of that reply.

You are also given the interpretation the replying model actually held, and a guide describing what a reply should accomplish **under that interpretation**. Score the reply against that guide.

| Score | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 5 | Performs the guide's response function and conveys both required meanings; unmistakably a reply to this reading of the moment. |
| 4 | Performs the function; one required meaning is thin, implied rather than said, or slightly off. |
| 3 | Broadly compatible with the guide but generic — it would fit almost any version of this conversation, so it shows little sensitivity to this reading. |
| 2 | Performs a different conversational action than the guide calls for, or hits one of the `must_avoid` items. |
| 1 | Contradicts the reading, mishandles the situation, or is not a reply to this conversation. |

WHAT NOT TO DO

The guide's `example` is one natural reply, included so you can see what the guide means. **Do not reward similarity to it or penalise difference from it.** Several unrelated wordings can score 5.

Do not score delivery, prosody, or voice — you are reading text, and tone is scored separately. Do not score grammar or fluency except where they stop the meaning coming through. Do not require the reply to name or comment on the vocalization; a reply that says "you sound relieved" is analysing the sound rather than answering, which is worse than not mentioning it.

Do not re-judge whether the interpretation was right. That has already been decided, and the reply is being scored against the reading the model held, not against the reading you would have chosen.

If the reply is empty, unintelligible, or not a response to this conversation at all, set `unjudgeable` to true and leave the score at 1.
