HOW TO ANSWER

Listen to the reply's delivery: its affective direction, its intensity, its stance toward the other person, its pitch movement, energy, rate, pausing and voice quality.

Then, for each listed tone, decide whether you actually hear it. Report only the ones you do.

There is no single correct delivery here, and the note on the acceptable range is there to stop you mistaking breadth for error. A reply that is restrained, or brief, or simply not what you would have chosen is not wrong. Report a tone only if a listener would hear it as a misreading of the moment.

Judge only how it sounds. Not whether the words are the right words — that is scored elsewhere. Not how pleasant the voice is, not whose voice it resembles, not general text-to-speech naturalness, and not recording noise or synthesis artifacts.

The reply does not have to mirror the original vocalization. Answering a laugh without laughing is not an error, and neither is the reverse.

If an artifact is severe enough that tone cannot be judged at all — silence, truncation, unintelligible audio — set `unjudgeable` to true and say why, rather than reporting tones you cannot actually hear.
