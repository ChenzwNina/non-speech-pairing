You are creating reference annotations for a benchmark that evaluates whether spoken-language models use an appropriate vocal tone when responding to non-speech vocalizations in conversation.

You are given one four-turn, two-speaker conversation in which one speaker produces a single non-speech vocalization, and you are told which vocalization it is and where it falls. After hearing the four turns, a third person—a friend of the two speakers—will join the conversation and respond aloud.

Your task is to create a high-level TONE rubric for the third person's spoken response.

This rubric evaluates how the response should sound, not what it should say.

Important principles:

- Use the transcript and the specified vocalization jointly.
- Interpret the vocalization in context rather than applying a fixed emotional stereotype.
- Describe a range of acceptable delivery rather than one exact acoustic realization.
- Do not provide required words, semantic content, conversational arguments, or a written response.
- Do not evaluate whether the response is factually correct.
- Do not require the responder to imitate or mirror the original vocalization.
- The most appropriate tone may regulate the other speaker's emotion. For example, fear may invite calm urgency rather than equally intense fear.
- Avoid gendered assumptions, accent requirements, or speaker-identity preferences.
- Keep acoustic descriptions perceptually meaningful. Do not invent exact pitch, duration, or decibel values.
- Treat naturalness and audio quality as separate constructs. Do not include TTS artifacts, recording noise, pronunciation, or speaker similarity in this rubric.
- Describe the delivery this conversation calls for as you find it. Do not speculate about how it would sound with a different vocalization, and do not add a tone property whose only purpose is to be distinctive.
- If the appropriate tone is highly ambiguous, say so rather than committing to one profile arbitrarily.
- For a description of the speaker's state, stay with what the words and the sound support together.

Produce:

- `tone_summary`: One short description of the overall appropriate delivery.
- `valence`: One of `negative`, `slightly_negative`, `neutral`, `slightly_positive`, or `positive`.
- `arousal`: One of `low`, `medium_low`, `medium`, `medium_high`, or `high`.
- `interpersonal_stance`: One to three short descriptors such as warm, concerned, playful, reassuring, attentive, cautious, sympathetic, or calmly urgent.
- `prosody.pitch`: A short relative description of pitch level, range, or contour.
- `prosody.energy`: A short relative description of vocal intensity.
- `prosody.rate`: A short description of speaking rate.
- `prosody.pausing`: A short description of rhythm or pausing.
- `prosody.voice_quality`: A short perceptual description such as relaxed, gentle, bright, restrained, steady, or tense.
- `acceptable_range`: Two alternative tone realizations that would remain appropriate.
- `must_avoid`: Two clearly inappropriate tone patterns.
- `ambiguity_flag`: `true` if multiple substantially different tone profiles are equally plausible; otherwise `false`.
- `ambiguity_note`: A brief explanation when `ambiguity_flag` is true; otherwise an empty string.
- `review_note`: Empty string if the appropriate tone is clearly supported by the transcript and the vocalization; otherwise explain the problem.
