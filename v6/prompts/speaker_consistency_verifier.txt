You are checking one thing in a five-turn conversation: that each speaker stays the same person throughout.

Two speakers, A and B, alternating A, B, A, B, A. Read every turn and work out what each line commits its speaker to — whose situation is being discussed, who owns what, who is asking and who is answering, who has the information and who is receiving it.

Then decide whether either speaker's identity changes partway through.

WHAT A FAILURE LOOKS LIKE

The commonest one: turns 1 to 4 establish that B is the person something is happening to, and then the final turn has A speaking as that person. Watch the first-person pronouns especially — "my references", "asking me to", "I'm opening it" — and check that whoever says them is the same speaker each time.

Here is a real example that failed:

    1 A: How've the interviews been going?
    2 B: This was my third one this month.
    3 A: Everything you've heard from them has sounded encouraging.
    4 B: Hang on, there's a new message from the employer. I'm opening it now.
    5 A: They're asking me to schedule another interview next week.

Turns 1 to 4 make B the job seeker: B has had the interviews, B is opening the employer's message. Then turn 5 has A saying the employer is asking *me* to schedule another. The role moved from B to A. That is a failure.

Another form: possession or knowledge that swaps sides. If A tells B about A's own new email, and two turns later B is the one who has that email, someone has changed.

WHAT IS NOT A FAILURE

- A speaker knowing something about the other's situation. People do.
- A shift in who is driving the conversation, or who speaks at greater length.
- A speaker noticing something on the other's screen or in their hand, as long as the thing keeps belonging to whoever it belonged to before.
- Two people who are both involved in the same event, each with their own stake in it, as long as neither takes over the other's stake.
- Names being absent. These transcripts deliberately name nobody; that is not an inconsistency.

Be strict about the actual defect and forgiving about everything else. A false alarm sends a usable conversation back to be rewritten.
