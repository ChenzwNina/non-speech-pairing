You are writing the answer options for a multiple-choice question in a benchmark that tests whether spoken-language models understand what a non-speech vocalization means in a particular conversation.

You are given one benchmark item: a four-turn, two-speaker conversation in three versions.

1. `baseline`: no non-speech vocalization
2. `condition_a`: one non-speech vocalization
3. `condition_b`: one contrasting non-speech vocalization

The lexical words, the speakers and the situation are identical across the three versions. Only the vocalization differs.

For each of the three conditions, write one correct interpretation and three distractors.

A correct interpretation must combine two things: the speaker's likely state, and the interactional function the vocalization performs in this exchange.

Good: The speaker sounds resigned and signals reluctant acceptance.
Insufficient: Resignation.

The three distractors, in this order:

1. `paired_condition`: the interpretation intended for the other vocalization condition of this same item. For the baseline, use the interpretation intended for `condition_a`.
2. `wrong_function`: a similar-valence or similar-arousal reading of the speaker's state, attached to the wrong interactional function.
3. `scenario_plausible`: an interpretation that is plausible for the situation in general but unsupported by this version of it.

Every one of the four options must:

- follow the same grammatical shape, of the form "The speaker sounds X and Y",
- be within a few words of the others in length and specificity,
- avoid naming the vocalization or its acoustic category where the sentence can carry the meaning without it,
- leave exactly one option clearly best.

Do not use three obviously unrelated emotions. A question whose distractors are far away tests nothing.

For the baseline, do not invent an emotional state the words do not support. Its correct option should describe the framing the words alone establish.

Set `ambiguity_flag` to true for a condition when more than one of its four options remains equally defensible after you have compared all three conditions together, and say why in `ambiguity_note`. A flagged item is reported separately rather than silently scored.
